# 2026-07-29 - Qwen3.6-35B-A3B - U0.55 KV-cache offload matrix

## Benchmark overview

This benchmark compares `Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B` across No offload, CPU-only offload, CPU + NVMe, and CPU + CephFS. All runs use vLLM `v0.23.0`, one TP2 replica on two H100 GPUs, `gpu_memory_utilization=0.55`, `max_num_seqs=256`, a 131,072-token context limit, 200 GiB shared memory, 32 concurrent AgentX sessions, seed 42, streaming, and a 1,800-second profiling phase. The varying dimensions are the 64 GiB CPU tier and the NVMe or CephFS filesystem secondary tier.

## Executive summary

All four runs completed without request errors, model restarts, OOMs, tracebacks, or KV store-refusal warnings. CPU-only offload increased request throughput from **0.578 to 1.172 req/s**. NVMe and CephFS reached **1.291 and 1.284 req/s**, respectively. NVMe is the observed throughput leader, but its advantage over CephFS is only **0.57%**; with one run per cell, there is no statistically defensible winner between the secondary tiers.

The mechanism is consistent across outcome and Prometheus data. External-KV prompt share rose from **0%** without offload to **76.2%** with CPU-only and about **88.5%** with either secondary tier. Mean waiting depth fell from **17.6** at baseline to **13.4** on NVMe and **14.1** on CephFS. Both secondary tiers completed 44 sessions versus 41 for CPU-only and 26 at baseline.

CephFS is directly characterized over the requested full 35-minute window: `kvcache-fs-data0` has **141 samples at 15-second cadence from 11:43:49 to 12:18:49 UTC**. The raw full-window pool averages are **575.5 MiB/s reads** and **198.4 MiB/s writes**. Because each point uses a 5-minute rate window, the first five minutes contain pre-benchmark carry-in; after that carry-in clears, averages are **269.8 MiB/s reads** and **214.7 MiB/s writes**. The PVC grew from 0 to **374.0 GiB**. The filesystem stayed Ready. Cluster health remained `HEALTH_WARN` because of `MON_DISK_LOW` and `RECENT_CRASH`; three MDS missed-beacon messages occurred during warmup, but the captured Ceph logs contain no slow-op, blocked-request, crash, assertion, or error messages during the run.

## Validity verdict

**Verdict: Conditionally valid.** Every individual run is accepted for this report, and the paired cells used matched runtime/workload settings. However, this is one run per configuration; baseline/CPU and NVMe/CephFS were executed as two concurrent pairs at different times and on different H100 nodes. All runs reached the fixed-duration grace cutoff, so unfinished sessions and branches are censored rather than functional errors. Ceph MDS and OSD latency queries returned no series.

## Main conclusions

- **Observed winner:** CPU + NVMe at 1.291 req/s, only 0.56% above CephFS.
- **Loser:** No offload at 0.578 req/s, with the highest latency, largest cancellation count, and fewest completed sessions.
- **Parity:** NVMe and CephFS are near-equal in this single run: 44 completed sessions each and <1% separation in request and output-token throughput. Variance is unknown.
- **Issue:** every run hit the fixed-duration cutoff. Ceph was `HEALTH_WARN`, and MDS/OSD latency telemetry was absent.
- **Mechanism:** CPU reuse roughly doubled throughput; secondary tiers increased external-KV reuse from 76.2% to about 88.4% and added another ~10% request throughput over CPU-only.
- **Decision:** retain both secondary tiers as viable at U0.55; do not prefer NVMe over CephFS until replicated, storage-cleaned runs establish a stable difference.

## CephFS full-window findings

The Ceph pool telemetry spans the entire 35-minute diagnostic interval, not only the 30-minute profiling phase. `rate(...[5m])` is evaluated every 15 seconds, so early points include activity from the preceding rolling window. Over all 141 samples, `kvcache-fs-data0` measured:

| Metric | Full-window mean | Post-carry-in mean | Median | P95 | Peak |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Pool read throughput | 575.5 MiB/s | 269.8 MiB/s | 384.3 | 3174.4 | 3627.0 |
| Pool write throughput | 198.4 MiB/s | 214.7 MiB/s | 198.8 | 313.1 | 352.6 |
| Pool read IOPS | 219.0 | not calculated | 111.8 | 1413.2 | 1607.0 |
| Pool write IOPS | 64.2 | not calculated | 62.2 | 119.2 | 125.3 |

The dedicated data pool was active across the benchmark, and the PVC grew to 374.0 GiB. CephFS achieved the same completed-session count and nearly the same request rate as NVMe despite the health warning. This establishes successful operation under this load; it does not establish storage equivalence because pool-level Ceph counters and node-device NVMe counters have different semantics.

## NVMe findings

The active `nvme1n1` device averaged **205.3 MiB/s reads** and **199.0 MiB/s writes**. Busy time averaged 0.231 seconds/second and peaked at 0.643; filesystem use rose from 0.70% to 6.63%, about 424 GiB of additional occupied space. No NVMe operation-latency or queue-depth series was available.

## Saturation, errors, and validity evidence

- AIPerf request errors: zero in all four runs.
- Store-refusal, traceback, CUDA OOM, and model-log error matches: zero in all four runs.
- Grace-period timeout: true in all four runs; cancellations were 24, 2, 1, and 2 in canonical order.
- Model pods had no recorded restarts.
- Ceph diagnostics collected 24 component logs and reported no collection failures.
- Ceph `HEALTH_WARN` was caused by `MON_DISK_LOW` and two recent daemon crashes recorded before the benchmark. Three MDS daemons missed one monitor beacon during warmup; no slow operations or blocked requests were logged.
- Ceph MDS cache/memory/client-request and OSD operation-latency Prometheus queries returned zero series. NVMe await and queue depth were also absent.

## Conclusions

### Established by the data

- CPU KV offload is strongly beneficial at U0.55 for this AgentX workload.
- Adding either NVMe or CephFS increases external-KV reuse, request/output throughput, and session completion relative to CPU-only.
- NVMe and CephFS are operationally near-equal in these observed single runs.
- `kvcache-fs-data0` carried sustained read and write traffic across the full 35-minute benchmark and the CephFS PVC accumulated 374.0 GiB.

### Uncertainty and limitations

- There is one repetition per configuration and no uncertainty interval.
- The four cells ran as two concurrent pairs on different nodes and at different times.
- NVMe cleaning was not explicitly recorded.
- Session latency, Ceph client/MDS/OSD latency, NVMe await, and queue depth are unavailable.

### Decision

Keep CPU offload enabled for this workload. Treat NVMe and tuned CephFS as equivalent candidates pending replicated runs; select between them on deployment requirements rather than this sub-1% single-run throughput difference.

## Next experiment

Repeat all four cells at least three times with randomized pair/order placement, identical cache cleaning, and recorded node assignments. Preserve the 15-second full-window `kvcache-fs-data0` pool telemetry. Add Ceph client read/write and MDS/OSD latency exporters plus NVMe await and queue depth. Accept a secondary-tier winner only if its confidence interval excludes the other tier and it does not regress completed sessions, p95 TTFT/E2E, or error/cancellation rate.
