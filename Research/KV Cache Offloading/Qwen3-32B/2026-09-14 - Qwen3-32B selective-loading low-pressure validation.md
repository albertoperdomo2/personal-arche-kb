# Qwen3-32B selective-loading low-pressure validation

This experiment asks whether the proposed combined selective-loading policy stays out of the way when the system is not under meaningful queue or storage pressure. It compares current external-loading behavior with the exact proposed configuration: load when at least 1,024 external tokens are reusable, close loading when the endpoint-local waiting-request EWMA reaches 8, and reopen at 4.

## Executive summary

At an 8K reusable-prefix workload and concurrency 4, the combined policy matched current behavior. It delivered 1.34% more output-token throughput, 3.80% lower mean TTFT, and 1.02% lower mean ITL, with zero errors in either run. These small single-run differences are evidence of no regression, not evidence of a low-pressure speedup.

External loading remained active. The combined run averaged 1,485 external prompt tokens/s and 477 MB/s of CPU-to-GPU KV traffic, compared with 1,149 tokens/s and 381 MB/s under current behavior. Archived endpoint queues averaged 0.14 request and peaked at 6, below the close threshold of 8. Active-node NVMe busy time averaged 32.0% and peaked at 51.3%.

## Validity verdict: Valid for low-pressure no-regression

The two configurations used the same model, workload, four TP2 replicas pinned to the same 8xH100 node, local NVMe tier, cache cleanup, runtime image, and EPP image. Both completed without request failures. The comparison supports the narrow conclusion that the combined policy does not penalize this low-pressure case.

The run does not prove that the EPP queue gate never fired. Prometheus archived queue state every 15 seconds, while the EPP consumes more frequent endpoint updates and maintains a two-second EWMA. No reason-labeled decision counter reconstructs individual actions.

## Main takeaways

- Measured: output-token throughput was 235.25 tokens/s with the combined policy and 232.14 tokens/s with current behavior.
- Measured: combined mean TTFT was 294.56 ms versus 306.20 ms; p99 TTFT was 637.32 ms versus 656.89 ms.
- Measured: combined mean ITL was 14.80 ms/token versus 14.96 ms/token; p99 ITL was effectively unchanged.
- Measured: all requests succeeded, external KV loading remained active, and archived endpoint queues stayed below 8.
- Inference: at low pressure, the combined policy preserves useful loading rather than broadly opting out to recomputation.
- Decision: use this as the low-pressure complement to the saturated matrix and describe it as a no-regression result.

## Configuration

| Configuration | Deployment profile | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Current behavior | `multi-tier-offloading-nvme` | External KV loading without a selective gate |
| Combined 1,024 + 8 | `rhoai-distributed-default-selective-loading-combined` | Require 1,024 external tokens; close at waiting EWMA 8 and reopen at 4 |

Both used Qwen3-32B, four TP2 replicas colocated on `diadochos-hqxzk-gpu-h100-mt46x`, a 256 GiB CPU tier, local NVMe with 64 read and 64 write threads, 300 GiB shared memory, precise-prefix routing, and `cleanup: true`. ## Limitations

- One sequential run per configuration means the small deltas do not establish an advantage.
- No reason-labeled decision telemetry reconstructs queue-veto activation.
- Queue and mechanism telemetry is archived at 15-second cadence.
- GPU utilization and GPU KV-cache utilization queries produced no usable samples.
- The result is specific to this Qwen3-32B TP2, four-replica H100 and local-NVMe deployment.
- Both EPP runs emitted tracing-export errors because no collector listened on `localhost:4317`. This was shared noise rather than a differential failure.

## Conclusion

The low-pressure experiment supplies the safety-side validation for the combined policy. When reusable external KV is valuable and endpoint pressure stays below the close threshold, the policy preserves loading and matches current behavior in throughput, TTFT, and ITL. Combined with the saturated matrix, this is consistent with the intended design: retain loading when it is cheap and veto it when restore pressure threatens serving.
